About

I started programming out of passion in high school and landed my first commercial programming job in Tehran in 2004, during my bachelor studies. Through the BSc and MSc years I worked part-time on Java, JavaScript, and C# projects, and in 2014 I moved to Berlin to work as a backend developer at the medical software startup Doc Cirrus.

Curiosity about the theory behind the systems I was building led me to a PhD. I worked on algorithms for traffic control in softwarized networks, supervised by Prof. Stefan Schmid in the Communication Technologies group at the University of Vienna, with results published at CoNEXT, INFOCOM, and OPODIS. Since then I have built software in three quite different industries: quantum computing, energy management, and semiconductor nanofabrication.

These days I write Python, TypeScript, and Swift, and I keep a steady stream of side projects going, many of them small native macOS tools. I'm most interested in roles that combine applied research with hands-on software development.

    Research

    My PhD asked a practical question: when a network is programmable, how do you steer traffic through it well, and how do you keep it flowing when links fail? I worked on traffic engineering with segment routing (how to split flows over paths so no link saturates), fast rerouting (precomputed detours that take over within microseconds of a failure), and online graph partitioning (keeping chatty processes on the same machine as communication patterns shift). The common thread is combinatorial optimization applied to real networking constraints.
